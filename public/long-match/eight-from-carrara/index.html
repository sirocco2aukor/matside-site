Eight From Carrara

One afternoon on the Gold Coast decided every seat Asia and Oceania will occupy in Kraków. Nobody has written about the people who won them. Here they are — and here are two errors in the existing record that we are correcting on the way through.

By The Cornerman Melbourne · 7 September 2026 Reading time 9 min

Melbourne — The ADCC World Championship opens in Kraków on Saturday with 104 competitors: five men's divisions of sixteen, three women's divisions of eight. North America and Europe each send athletes through multiple trials events. Asia and Oceania — a region running from Kazakhstan to New Zealand, taking in Japan, Korea, China, Hong Kong and Australia on the way — get one.

It was held on 21 June at People First Stadium in Carrara, on the Gold Coast, and it lasted a day. Eight people won, and those eight are the region's entire representation at the world championship of their sport.

Six of them have never competed at an ADCC World Championship before. In the eleven weeks since, the English-language grappling press has produced, between all of its outlets, a results list and roughly three paragraphs of analysis. Two of the eight have been misidentified in print and the errors are still standing.

This is the whole reason Matside publishes from this side of the world. What follows is each of the eight, checked against ADCC's own official results rather than against another outlet's report, with what they did in Carrara and what they walk into on Saturday.

Corrections to the public record

Two errors carried by other outlets since 22 June. Neither has been corrected at source.

01

The −55kg champion is Kanae Ikeda, not Kanae Yamada

Jits Magazine's results report of 22 June names the women's −55kg champion as "Kanae Yamada" and repeats the surname five times. ADCC's own official results for the event list her as Kanae Ikeda, Japan, of IGLOO. FloGrappling's recap, filed the same day, also has Ikeda.

She is the only athlete from East Asia in the region's entire delegation to Kraków, and the outlet that gave her the most coverage got her name wrong.

Verified against ADCC official results · Matside § 2.4

02

Kaya Rudolph is a New Zealander

FloGrappling's list of champions from the trials records the −77kg winner as Australian. ADCC's own official results record him as New Zealand, of Absolute MMA. The association is Australian and Rudolph trains out of its Melbourne room, which is the likely source of the error — the body of the same FloGrappling article identifies him only as a student of that room and gives no nationality at all.

The error has a consequence the outlet has not addressed. The same article calls +99kg winner Belal Etiabari "the first athlete from New Zealand to win an ADCC Trials event". That claim holds only if Rudolph is Australian. On ADCC's record he is not — two New Zealanders won trials titles in the same building on the same afternoon, and the claim needs a qualifier nobody has supplied.

Verified against ADCC official results · Matside § 2.4 · this correction was itself corrected — see below

Correction to correction 02

10 September 2026. As published on 7 September, correction 02 stated that FloGrappling described Rudolph as Australian “in its body text, while the athlete list at the top of the same article says New Zealand”. That is the wrong way round: the champions list gives Australia, and the body text gives no nationality. The finding is unchanged — ADCC's official record says New Zealand, and the “first New Zealander” claim rests on the error. The original wording is quoted here rather than deleted.

The eight

Team and nationality as recorded in ADCC's official results. The men enter sixteen-athlete brackets — three wins to a medal. The women enter brackets of eight — two.

M −66

Bekzat Kapashov

Five wins, three by submission, for a Kazakh grappling programme that has gone from invisible to routinely competitive inside about five years. Kapashov competes for the ADCC Kazakhstan national team rather than a private academy — a structure that looks more like wrestling or judo than like the gym-and-affiliation model the rest of this bracket comes from.

He beat Canada's David Stoilescu in the final. Australia's Liam Urwin took third.

Kanae Ikeda

The only athlete from East Asia in the region's delegation, and the one whose name the coverage got wrong. Ikeda won four matches and took the final against one of the division favourites — Margot Ciccarelli, competing for Hong Kong out of Art of Jiu-Jitsu, who has spent years as one of the most recognised names on this circuit.

The finding

One Melbourne association is a quarter of the delegation

Read ADCC's official results by team rather than by nation and a different picture appears. Absolute MMA — the association Lachlan Giles leads from St Kilda in Melbourne — has two of the eight athletes going to Kraków, and two more on the podium behind them.

−77kgKaya Rudolph — 1st · qualified
+99kgBelal Etiabari — 1st · qualified
−77kgSiraj Soufi — 3rd
−66kgLiam Urwin — 3rd

Giles is the man who in 2019 became the first Australian to medal in the ADCC absolute division, heel-hooking three men far heavier than himself, and who was on the other side of the 2022 absolute bracket that Yuri Simões won — the same Simões who headlines Saturday's superfight in Kraków.

Twenty-five per cent of a region's world championship delegation coming from one association is not a regional development story. It is a single room doing work that federations across the rest of the region are not doing, and it is eight kilometres from this desk.

How this was checked

Every name, nationality, team and placing above was taken from ADCC's official results page for the 2nd ADCC Asia & Oceania Trials 2026, held 21 June 2026 at People First Stadium, Carrara QLD, and not from another outlet's report. Match counts, finish counts and the 11–0 final score come from the recaps published by FloGrappling (Joe Gilpin, 22 June 2026) and Jits Magazine (22 June 2026), and are attributed as such where they appear.

Where those two accounts conflict with the official record, the official record is used and the conflict is reported rather than quietly resolved. Where a claim cannot be settled from the public record — the order of Saturday's finals in Carrara, and therefore the "first New Zealander" question — it is left open.

Coverage of Matilda Reid is limited under § 3.3 of the Matside charter, which restricts reporting on competitors under eighteen to what the bracket itself records. That clause was amended on 10 September, after this article breached the version published with it; both the amendment and the reason are logged in Standards & Practice.
