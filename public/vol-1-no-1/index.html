Exclusive · ADCC World Championship · Kraków, 12–13 September

Eight From Carrara

One afternoon on the Gold Coast decided every seat Asia and Oceania will occupy at the world championship of their sport. Eleven weeks later, two of the eight are still misidentified in print. Matside has checked all of them against the official record.

By The Cornerman · Matside · Melbourne

Melbourne — The ADCC World Championship opens in Kraków on Saturday with 104 competitors. North America and Europe each send athletes through multiple trials events. Asia and Oceania — a region running from Kazakhstan to New Zealand, taking in Japan, Korea, China and Hong Kong on the way — get one. It was held on 21 June at Carrara on the Gold Coast, it lasted a day, and the eight people who won it are the region's entire representation.

Six of them have never competed at an ADCC World Championship. In the eleven weeks since, the English-language grappling press has produced, between all of its outlets, a results list and about three paragraphs of analysis.

It also produced two errors that are still standing. The women's −55kg champion — the only athlete from East Asia in the delegation — has been named in print as Kanae Yamada. ADCC's official results say Kanae Ikeda, of IGLOO, Japan. And the −77kg champion, Kaya Rudolph, appears as Australian in FloGrappling's list of champions. ADCC's official record says New Zealand.

The second error has a consequence. The same article calls the +99kg winner the first New Zealander ever to take an ADCC Trials title — a claim that needs a qualifier if, as ADCC records, a second New Zealander won one in the same building on the same afternoon.

Read the official results by team rather than by nation and something else appears. Absolute MMA — the association Lachlan Giles leads from St Kilda, eight kilometres from this desk — has two of the eight qualifiers and two more on the podium behind them. A quarter of a region's world championship delegation came out of one Melbourne association.

That is not a regional development story. It is one room doing work that federations across the rest of the region are not doing, and nobody has written it down until now.

Corrected 10 September 2026. As published on 7 September this article stated that FloGrappling described Kaya Rudolph as Australian in its body text while the athlete list in the same article said New Zealand. It is the other way round: the champions list gives Australia, and the body text gives no nationality at all. ADCC's official record says New Zealand, and the finding is unchanged. § 4.1 — The Cornerman

Also on the card

The first Worlds without him

Gordon Ryan announced his retirement in February, citing long-running gut and immune problems, and vacated the superfight title he had held since 2022. He is thirty and he is not on the card. In his place ADCC has matched the last two absolute champions — Kaynan Duarte, who won the open class in 2024, against Yuri Simões, who won it in 2022 through a bracket that included Melbourne's Lachlan Giles.

It is the first ADCC World Championship staged in Europe since 2017 and the largest room the event has been given: Tauron Arena Kraków seats roughly 22,000. A sport that is genuinely deep does not notice when one athlete leaves. A sport built on one gravitational body notices immediately. On Saturday we find out which one this is.

The Corner · Founding editorial

We are starting a newspaper at the edge of the mat

The Cornerman Editor · Melbourne · 7 September 2026

Melbourne — Here is the arrangement as it stands. FloSports holds exclusive worldwide media rights to ADCC — all eight trials, the Amateur World Championship, and the World Championship itself, extended through this year. FloGrappling, the same company, also operates the largest editorial desk in grappling. Its annual subscription is US$150. Much of the rest of what you can read about this sport is written by sites that carry the affiliate link to that subscription at the bottom of the page.

I want to be exact, because exactness is the point: none of that is a scandal, and none of it is illegal. FloSports has done more to make this sport visible than any newspaper ever did. But a company that sells the event cannot also be the paper of record for the event, and everybody in grappling has quietly agreed to pretend otherwise because the alternative was nothing at all.

Roughly US$20 million in prize money and investment has been announced for this sport inside eighteen months — the UFC's stated $10–12 million commitment, Craig Jones's $10 million purse. One of those has produced eight events. The other has produced a press release and, five months on, no date. Nobody independent is keeping the ledger.

So: Matside. We are based in Melbourne, which is not an accident. This city has supported a daily broadsheet since 1854 and has produced, in Absolute MMA St Kilda, one of the most consequential grappling rooms in the world — the room that made Lachlan Giles, who in 2019 became the first Australian to medal in the ADCC absolute by heel-hooking three men far bigger than him. Melbourne also sits inside the region ADCC compresses into a single trials afternoon. We are not covering the rounding error from outside it. We live in it.

We own no rights. We sell no tickets. We take no cut of anything that happens on the mat. We sit at the edge of it, outside the line, and we write down what happened.
